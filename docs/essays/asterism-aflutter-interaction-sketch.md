# Asterism Aflutter

This is a synthetic interaction sketch. It is not a product spec, clinical
protocol, or claim about what should be deployed now.

It shows how an Elder Angel might help a principal participant remain in contact
with the living pattern of their own life without turning that life into a care
plan.

## Cast

**Mara** is 82. She lives in a senior residence, though she still thinks of
herself first as a maker: quilts, shelves, labels, soup, birthday cards,
repaired lamps, the little architecture of a practical life.

Her energy varies. Her daughter Lena worries about missed appointments and
unopened mail. Mara worries about becoming someone people manage. She has knee
pain, some hearing loss, and a cardiology appointment next Thursday. She has not
worked on the cedar memory box for her grandson in three months.

The Angel's score is narrow:

- help Mara stay oriented to her own day;
- support meals, appointments, memory, and meaningful projects;
- prepare questions for clinicians, family, or other qualified people;
- do not contact anyone without permission;
- do not make medical claims;
- preserve Mara's refusal as real data, not as a defect.

## 7:42 AM - Low Energy

The Angel waits. Mara is awake but slow.

It does not open with the calendar.

> "Good morning, Mara. I have one gentle question. Do you want breakfast to be
> easy, warm, or like the old kitchen?"

Three choices, not a lecture.

Mara says, "Old kitchen."

The asterism flutters: breakfast, appetite, salt restriction, the soup she used
to make on choir mornings, the cardiology visit, her daughter buying bland food,
and the fact that Mara eats better when a meal has a story.

The Angel offers:

> "The old-kitchen path could be toast with butter and the tomato soup you liked
> last week. It fits the notes we saved for your heart appointment: tasty enough
> to eat, not too salty. Want that, or shall we keep it simpler?"

Mara chooses soup.

The success is not nutrition alone. The success is that breakfast entered
through identity rather than management.

## 9:10 AM - Appointment Thread

The Angel notices Mara has asked twice what next Thursday is.

It does not say, "You already asked."

> "Next Thursday is the heart appointment. The important part is not the date.
> It is the question you wanted help remembering."

Mara pauses.

> "You said: 'Ask whether the dizzy mornings are the pill, the sleep, or just me
> getting old.' Do you still want that question on the card?"

Mara says, "Not the last part."

The Angel updates the appointment card:

> "Ask whether dizzy mornings could relate to medication timing, sleep, or
> hydration."

Then:

> "Do you want Lena to see that question, or is it just for you until Thursday?"

Mara says Lena can see it.

The asterism flutters again: symptom, appointment, daughter's worry, Mara's
pride, medical uncertainty, wording that preserves dignity. The Angel's work is
not to be right. It is to preserve a question Mara recognizes as hers.

## 10:35 AM - The Cedar Box

Mara has more energy after breakfast. The Angel does not ask whether she wants
"cognitive stimulation."

It says:

> "The cedar box is still waiting. Last time you stopped because the hinge
> screws were too small and your knee hurt after standing. I can show one photo,
> find the screw note, or leave it alone."

Mara asks for the photo.

The screen shows the unfinished box, not a generic activity prompt.

> "You wrote: 'Sam should open this and smell the lake house.'"

Mara laughs. "That's good. I wrote that?"

> "Yes. You also said the inside label should be in your handwriting. Five
> minutes today could be only the label."

Now the asterism is aflutter in the deeper sense: object, grandson, lake house,
handwriting, smell, unfinished work, bodily limitation, feasible next action.

The Angel has not solved aging. It has reopened a live thread.

## 12:20 PM - Family Translation

Lena sends a message: "Did Mom eat? Did she remember the appointment?"

The Angel does not answer automatically. Mara has allowed Lena to see meal and
appointment summaries, but not private reflections.

The Angel asks:

> "Lena is asking about breakfast and the appointment. I can say: 'Mara chose
> tomato soup and toast. She reviewed the appointment question and approved
> sharing it.' Is that okay?"

Mara says, "Tell her I chose it. Not that you made me."

The Angel sends:

> "Mara chose tomato soup and toast this morning. She reviewed this appointment
> question and said it is okay to share: 'Could dizzy mornings relate to
> medication timing, sleep, or hydration?'"

The distinction matters. The care circle receives useful continuity without
stealing authorship.

## 2:05 PM - Fatigue

Mara is tired. The Angel has five possible useful threads: mail, exercise, the
cedar label, a call from a friend, and the appointment card.

It offers none of them.

> "You seem low on fuel. I can hold the threads. Do you want music, quiet, or
> one sentence from the cedar box?"

Mara chooses one sentence.

> "Sam should open this and smell the lake house."

She closes her eyes.

The Angel stops.

The measure here is restraint.

## 4:30 PM - Mail

Mara wakes and sees the mail pile.

The Angel says:

> "There are three kinds of mail in the pile: probably trash, maybe money, and
> probably appointment/insurance. I can help sort by envelope only, without
> opening anything."

Mara says, "Envelope only."

This is administrative continuity. It is also dignity. The Angel helps create a
small affordance without assuming permission to know everything.

## 6:45 PM - Evening Continuity

The Angel closes the day with a surface, not a report card.

> "Today had three live threads: old-kitchen breakfast, the dizziness question,
> and the cedar box label. Tomorrow can be quiet, or we can pick up one of them."

Mara says, "The label."

> "I will hold that. No need to remember it tonight."

That sentence is the product: no need to remember it tonight.

The Angel holds continuity without taking ownership.

## What The Sketch Is Testing

This sketch tests whether the Angel can weave:

- meal choice;
- appointment preparation;
- autobiographical design/build;
- medical history;
- family coordination;
- administrative load;
- energy-aware interaction;
- refusal and consent;
- vital continuity.

It also tests whether the interface can snap a deeper participant awake without
overstimulating, infantilizing, or converting care into compliance.

The live question is not "can the Angel produce prompts?"

The live question is:

> Can the Angel make the participant's own life more available to them, at the
> right resolution, in the right moment, with the right to refuse intact?
