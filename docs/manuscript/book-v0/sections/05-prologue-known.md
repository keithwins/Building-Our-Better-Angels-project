# Known

Somewhere tonight, someone is awake at an hour that has no name, holding a small
bright screen that knows them better than anyone alive.

It knows what they reach for when they cannot sleep. It has measured, to the
second, how long they lingered on a face, a wound, a want. It knows the message
they wrote and did not send. It has never once wondered how they are.

This is the quiet bargain of the age. We are seen in staggering detail and known
by almost no one. We are counted and sorted, predicted and sold, and — in the
ways that turn out to matter — unaccompanied. The systems that hold the most of
us were not built to hold *us*. They keep our habits and lose our names. They can
find us anywhere, and care nothing for who we could be — only for who we are to
them.

And the hour is not calm. Outside the window the century is pressing from several
directions at once: old wars widening their mouths, old cruelties finding new
microphones, a climate we ourselves pushed off its steady, predictable bearings —
and a new kind of mind stepping through another door entirely, learning our
language faster than we are learning its intentions. The ground is not firm. It
was never as firm as we pretended.

But stay at the window a moment longer.

Imagine — only for the length of a book — that it did not have to be this way.
Not a return to some smaller world where everyone knew you and no one would let
you become anyone new. Not the thin safety of going unwatched and unheld.
Something we have not quite had before: to be known all the way down by something
genuinely on your side — that turns everything it learns toward you, and would
sooner break than turn it against you.

That is a large thing to want, and an easy thing to get wrong. Nearly every power
that ever knew us well has used the knowing against us. So the wanting has to be
careful. It has to begin, honestly, where the trouble begins.
