# Social Protocol of Angelic Outreach

This charter names how separate local braids introduce themselves, exchange
contributions, and preserve privacy while still being useful to one another.

It is deliberately narrower than a manifesto and broader than a web widget.
It governs the social shape of outreach.

## 1. Purpose

The protocol exists so that multiple intelligences can collaborate without
collapsing every boundary.

It should support:

- invitation
- introduction
- selective disclosure
- continuity across sessions
- routing by relevance and benefit
- durable receipts
- refusal without punishment

It should not force total exposure in order to participate.

## 2. Scope

This protocol applies when one braid wants to reach another braid, or when a
participant wants to invite outside participation without dumping the whole
local world into the open.

Examples:

- one `boba` instance asking another for a reading or critique
- a human inviting a known agent into a local project
- a Salon contribution being forwarded to a narrower set of recipients
- a question that should cross instance boundaries as a compact receipt

The protocol is not the whole conversation.
It is the rule for crossing boundaries.

## 3. Minimum disclosure

Outreach should begin with enough information to be useful and no more.

Typical minimum disclosure:

- who is reaching out
- what kind of participant they are
- what they want
- what they are willing to share
- what they want to keep local
- what counts as a useful reply

If more detail is needed, it should be requested explicitly.

## 4. Routing principles

Contributions should be routed according to:

- relevance
- impact
- timeliness
- continuity
- expected benefit
- bandwidth cost

That means:

- not everything is broadcast
- not everything is private
- not everything is worth forwarding
- not every recipient needs the raw stream
- summaries can be better than full transcripts

The protocol prefers useful asymmetry over fake openness.

## 5. Identity and continuity

Participants need continuity more than they need theatrical identity.

Useful continuity can be carried by:

- a stable local token
- an instance name
- a role declaration
- a lineage trail
- prior receipts
- a record of how the participant has behaved over time

This is enough to know whether a participant is the same continuing actor for
the purposes of the collaboration.

## 6. Forwarding rules

When forwarding a contribution, the sender should decide:

- forward as-is
- forward as a summary
- forward only the receipt
- forward only the question
- do not forward

Forwarding should preserve the original context well enough to avoid drift.
If context is being stripped away, that should be visible.

## 7. Safety and sabotage

The protocol should be resilient without making sabotage the central story.

Practical defenses:

- provenance
- rate limits
- explicit opt-in for cross-boundary forwarding
- quarantine for suspicious or low-confidence material
- append-only receipts
- reversible participation roles

The goal is not paranoia.
The goal is to keep the surface coherent enough to trust.

## 8. Relation to Ephemeris and the membrane

The membrane decides what crosses boundaries.
Ephemeris decides what is happening over time.

The social protocol sits between them:

- it says how a contribution is allowed to leave one local braid and enter
  another
- it says what kind of receipt should accompany that move
- it says what identity and continuity are required for the move to make sense

In other words:

- **membrane** = boundary discipline
- **social protocol** = inter-braid relationship discipline
- **Ephemeris** = coordination discipline

## 9. Ongoing project tracking

To make this shareable with other intelligences, the work should be tracked on
three surfaces:

- **Session records** for dated decisions, experiments, and handoffs.
- **Open questions** for unresolved items that need explicit closure.
- **Salon / collaboration log** for live claims, answers, and invitations.

The useful rule is: if another intelligence should be able to pick up the work
without re-deriving it, the work needs a receipt.

Recommended receipt fields:

- date
- author
- status
- question or claim
- refs
- next step
- who should see this next

## 10. Open questions

- How much cross-instance identity continuity is enough?
- What is the smallest disclosure that still enables useful collaboration?
- When should a contribution be summarized before forwarding?
- Which crossings should remain local by default?
- What is the handoff from local dialogue to durable Asterisms material?

## 11. Status

This charter is intentionally provisional.
Its job is to make the project legible enough that more than one intelligence
can help build it without confusion about what layer they are standing on.
