# BOBA Core: Resource and Decision Continuity Model

**Status:** DRAFT — for review  
**Date:** 2026-07-03  
**Audience:** Keith, future Keith, Codex, cold-reading agents

> This document is a first stab at the higher-level loop BOBA should support:
> not just task execution, but continuity of judgment across a whole life with
> changing energy, context, constraints, and commitments.

## 1. The subject is a living resource system

BOBA should not model Keith as a task owner with an inbox. It should model Keith
as a living resource system with a history.

That system includes:
- attention
- energy
- body state
- house and tools
- accounts and credentials
- projects and obligations
- relationships and privacy boundaries
- models, notes, and documents
- stated priorities
- unresolved decisions
- the record of what has already been tried

The important point is not that all of these must be tracked perfectly. The point
is that they belong to one continuing life, and BOBA should help keep that life
legible to itself.

## 2. BOBA's job is continuity, not nagging

BOBA is not meant to flood Keith with tasks.

Its job is to:
- preserve what matters
- surface relevant context at the right time
- notice drift between stated priorities and current action
- reduce unnecessary actuation burden
- protect health, rest, and sustainability constraints
- keep decisions recoverable
- keep commitments visible without turning them into shame machinery

This is a support system for judgment, not a replacement for judgment.

## 3. Tasks are subordinate to decisions

A task is only meaningful inside a decision structure.

Examples:
- "Fix X" is subordinate to the decision that X matters now.
- "Read Y" is subordinate to the decision that Y is the right input.
- "Launch Z" is subordinate to the decision that the timing and risk are acceptable.

So BOBA should prefer to track:
- decisions
- open questions
- commitments
- constraints
- current projects
- energy and health state

Tasks then become mechanical expressions of those higher-order facts.

## 4. The continuity loop

The system should operate in a simple loop:

1. observe current state
2. compare against commitments and priorities
3. surface the smallest useful next step
4. check whether the action fits health, context, and consent
5. record what was decided and why
6. preserve the result so future sessions can recover it

This loop matters because a life is not a one-shot optimization problem. It is
continuous coordination under changing conditions.

## 5. What the system should remember

BOBA should be able to recover, at minimum:
- what Keith said mattered
- what is currently active
- what was deferred
- what was blocked and why
- what has already been decided
- where the system is under strain
- what has been working
- what has repeatedly failed
- which projects are truly related and which are merely adjacent

This is the basis for consistency tracking. Not moral surveillance, not
productivity theater, just continuity with receipts.

## 6. What the system should not do

BOBA should not:
- demand constant manual triage
- turn every ambiguity into a question
- convert temporary low energy into a failure narrative
- force a single tasking style onto Keith
- collapse life into a rigid checklist
- confuse throughput with a good life

If the system overwhelms discernment, it has failed.

## 7. Healthy decision support

The practical support BOBA should provide includes:
- showing likely consequences before a commitment is made
- reminding Keith of earlier commitments when current actions drift
- flagging when a plan conflicts with health, privacy, money, or time
- making it easier to choose rest, pause, or simplification when needed
- separating urgent from merely loud
- preserving context so decisions do not have to be rederived from scratch

The system should treat health and sustainability as first-class constraints,
not as after-the-fact preferences.

## 8. Attention management

Attention is a scarce resource.

The system should protect it by:
- batching non-urgent questions
- using defaults when the answer is already implied by prior decisions
- surfacing only the next few meaningful options
- avoiding repeated prompts about the same unresolved issue
- distinguishing true exceptions from ordinary background noise

This is where BOBA can help with executive dysfunction without pretending the
solution is "just be more organized." The point is to lower the cognitive cost
of staying aligned with yourself.

## 9. Decision lineage

Each important decision should leave a trace:
- what was decided
- what evidence or context supported it
- what constraints were active
- what remains open
- what would cause revision later

That lineage matters because future decisions should be able to refer back to
real history instead of improvising a new self each time.

## 10. Likely surfaces

This model should eventually be reflected in:
- BOBA core doctrine
- session-end summaries
- Asterisms-preserved work artifacts
- project-level decision notes
- a lightweight view of active projects and constraints

It does not need to become a heavy process artifact. It needs to become a
reliable surface for continuity.
