## A way to enter, digest, and traverse a living corpus

Working proposition

A large, living corpus should not require every new reader, or its own author
returning after time away, to begin by reading everything, reconstructing
history, or trusting a flattened summary.

Instead, the corpus should be able to produce an Orientation Surface: a
temporary, situated presentation that helps someone understand what matters for
the question they have now.

An Orientation Surface is not merely a summary. It is a guided act of situated
sensemaking.

It may take the form of a brief, a narrated tour, a project map, a
podcast-like conversation, a reading path, a decision memo, an annotated
timeline, a visual landscape, or an interactive exchange. Its shape should
depend on who is arriving, what they are trying to understand, how much time
they have, and what kind of relationship they need to form with the material.

The goal is not to compress the corpus until it becomes simple. The goal is to
make it possible to enter complexity without being buried by it.

## Why this is needed

Most bodies of work accumulate in a form that is difficult even for their own
makers to revisit.

There are foundational documents, current implementation notes, abandoned
branches, vivid conversations, half-formed inventions, contradictory drafts,
source files, generated artifacts, task logs, private reflections, experimental
code, and materials whose importance is not yet clear.

A conventional overview usually fails in one of two ways:

It becomes so broad that it says almost nothing.

It becomes so selective that it falsely implies that the omitted material does
not matter.

An Orientation Surface should avoid both failures.

It should make clear:

what is active now,

what is foundational but not current,

what is real and implemented,

what is proposed or speculative,

what is dormant but worth preserving,

what conflicts or remains unresolved,

and where a reader should go next if they want to understand more.

It should never pretend that a project is smaller, cleaner, or more settled
than it is.

## The basic interaction

An Orientation Surface may begin with a small conversation.

Not an interrogation. Not a form. Just enough to understand the reader's
arrival.

Possible opening questions include:

What are you trying to understand right now?

Are you arriving as a collaborator, critic, investor, friend, future self,
technical implementer, or curious outsider?

Do you want the big picture, current status, unresolved questions, practical
next steps, or the strange and generative edges?

How much time do you have: two minutes, fifteen minutes, an hour, or an
open-ended exploration?

What do you already know?

Do you want a coherent route through the material, or an honest map of the
whole mess?

The system may also infer some of this from context, but it should always make
its assumptions visible and revisable.

A person who asks, "What is BOBA?" should not receive the same orientation as a
person who asks, "What has actually been implemented in Asterisms this week?"
Nor should either receive the same thing as someone asking, "What are Keith's
most promising independent engineering ideas?"

## Modes

An Orientation Surface may have several recognizable modes.

1. Panorama

A high-level map of the whole corpus.

This is the closest thing to an everything dump, but it should still be
organized around trunks, branches, relationships, and status rather than merely
listing files.

Useful for:

returning after a long absence,

onboarding a collaborator,

locating neglected projects,

recovering a sense of the whole.

2. Thread

A guided route through one idea across documents, projects, and time.

For example:

the evolution of trust beyond audit,

the development of Asterisms,

how Understory became more than transcription,

the relationship between BOBA and Vital Continuity,

the history of an invention or design line.

Useful for:

philosophical digestion,

research,

recovering why a decision was made,

seeing how a concept changed.

3. Current State

A practical briefing on what is actually true now.

This should privilege:

current repositories,

current commits,

current task boards,

tests and verification,

known blockers,

next actions,

recent changes.

Useful for:

resuming implementation,

agent handoffs,

work planning,

avoiding stale assumptions.

4. Decision Surface

A focused presentation for a choice that needs to be made.

It should include:

the decision,

options,

relevant history,

constraints,

unresolved assumptions,

consequences,

what evidence would change the answer,

and a clear separation between facts, interpretations, and recommendations.

Useful for:

design choices,

technical architecture,

property decisions,

safety issues,

resource allocation.

5. Deep Dive

A curated, slower entry into one project or concept.

This may include:

a short framing essay,

primary source excerpts,

linked artifacts,

a glossary,

contradictions,

questions worth carrying forward,

and suggested next reading.

Useful for:

serious collaborators,

writing,

research,

future self-reorientation.

6. Everything Dump

A deliberately broad, candid project-and-artifact register.

This mode says:

Here is what appears to exist. Here is what seems active. Here is what may be
lost. Here is what is connected. Here is what I cannot yet verify.

It is useful when the first task is not elegant understanding but recovery.

The phrase "everything dump" may remain as an informal internal name for this
mode, because it is honest about its function. But it should not be the only or
default experience of the corpus.

7. Tour / Podcast / Conversational Walkthrough

A spoken or podcast-like orientation for someone who may not want to read a
long briefing.

This could be:

a narrated guided tour,

a dialogue between two perspectives,

a sequence of short audio chapters,

or an interactive walk in which the listener interrupts and changes direction.

Useful for:

screen fatigue,

reflection while walking or driving,

introducing complicated work without handing someone a document mountain.

## What an Orientation Surface must preserve

An Orientation Surface may simplify presentation. It must not simplify reality
by hiding the conditions of its own construction.

It should preserve, or make available:

links to the underlying sources,

the distinction between source material and derivative interpretation,

dates and recency,

uncertainty,

disagreement,

conflicting versions,

the difference between implemented work and aspiration,

the difference between a durable commitment and a passing conversational idea,

and the identity of the perspective from which the surface was composed.

A surface may say:

This is a provisional synthesis made for someone trying to understand the
current Asterisms/Porter state. It emphasizes recent implementation evidence and
may underrepresent philosophical material.

That is much more trustworthy than pretending to be the corpus itself.

## What it must not become

An Orientation Surface should not become:

a single official summary that replaces the corpus,

a persuasion machine,

an authority that silently decides which material matters,

a cleanup operation that erases productive contradiction,

a forced taxonomy,

a substitute for source access,

or a system that treats a reader's current interest as the whole meaning of the
work.

The corpus remains plural, historical, unfinished, and capable of surprising
both its author and its readers.

The surface is a temporary landing place.

## Relation to Asterisms

Asterisms can provide the conditions that make Orientation Surfaces trustworthy.

It can retain:

original materials,

source identity,

provenance,

relations,

derivations,

dates,

access boundaries,

uncertainty,

and the fact that two things may be related without being identical.

An Orientation Surface should therefore be able to say not only "here are five
documents," but:

this is a source recording;

this transcript is a derivative;

this technical specification emerged from several conversations;

this implementation claim is supported by a current repository state;

this concept is older but still generative;

this proposal has never been tested;

this project is dormant, not dead.

The surface is not the archive. It is an act of navigation across the archive
and the living work.

## Relation to BOBA

For BOBA, Orientation Surfaces are not merely a documentation convenience.

They enact several central commitments:

support without replacement,

frame-opening rather than premature closure,

continuity without flattening,

assistance that enlarges agency,

respect for partial and uneven understanding,

and the refusal to make people reconstruct their own history alone.

A person should be able to say:

Help me understand what I have been doing.

Or:

Help me enter this body of work without pretending I already belong in it.

Or:

Show me the part that matters for this decision, but do not hide the larger
context.

That is a real form of assistance.

## First practical version

The first version does not need a sophisticated interface.

It can be a simple reusable protocol:

Identify the corpus, project, or question.

Ask two to five orientation questions.

Gather relevant source, implementation, and history material.

State what evidence was used and what could not be checked.

Produce one tailored surface:

written brief,

reading route,

current-state memo,

project map,

narrated outline,

or interactive conversation.

Preserve the output as a derivative artifact only when it is useful enough to
retain.

Keep the original corpus and source relationships intact.

The first Everything Dump version can become one of the first test cases.

It should be revised not by trying to make it exhaustive in the abstract, but
by asking:

Did this help a real person understand the terrain they came to understand?

## Open questions

Should an Orientation Surface be saved by default, or only when it becomes a
meaningful artifact?

How much should it adapt to the reader's prior knowledge?

How should it distinguish what Keith believes, what the corpus proposes, what
has been implemented, and what an assistant infers?

How should private, sensitive, or access-controlled material appear in a surface
without leaking or flattening its governance?

When should a surface expose contradictions directly, and when should it merely
signal that they exist?

Can a spoken orientation retain enough source traceability to remain
trustworthy?

What does a genuinely good returning after six months surface look like?

What would make a surface feel like companionship rather than a dashboard?

## Working conclusion

The point is not to generate better summaries.

The point is to make a living body of work inhabitable.

An Orientation Surface should let someone arrive with a real question, find a
route into the material, understand what is alive and what is uncertain, and
leave with a clearer sense of where they are, without being tricked into
believing that the terrain has been exhausted.
